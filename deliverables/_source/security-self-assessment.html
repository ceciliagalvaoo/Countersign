Confirmation, verification and rollback mechanisms

  • Confirmation. Every Red operation is shown on the approval card first, decoded from the exact bytes, with the network stated separately; the browser decodes a second time and shows Do not sign if its reading differs. Her wallet opens only after she presses Sign in your wallet.
  • Verification. The API decodes every confirmed transaction (contract, method, origin, arguments, the decision) before it changes its database. The screen says pending, not done until the ledger closes, and awaiting issuer countersignature until the mint is verified. The evidence export recomputes every chain and fails on any divergence.
  • Time bound. A policy approval is valid for 15 minutes; at execution the policy evaluates the proposal again and re-reads the mandate hash, caps, spending and revocation on chain (decision_expired otherwise).
  • Interrupt. A recurring purchase waits in a visible window before execution; Interrupt stops it, and the plan starts no execution until she resumes.
  • Rollback. A settled transaction cannot be reversed, so the design stops harm before and after it: she can revoke_agent (the contract then refuses the agent) and withdraw her USDC; the issuer can set_pause the fund units, and refunds a paid order it does not approve, so no investor loses funds to an order that is never minted.

Section 03

03Data and Privacy

Data provenance, consent, minimisation, storage and deletion

Topic What we do
Provenance Her chat messages and the mandate she approves; the demonstration fund catalogue we seeded (simulated funds and debtors, synthetic concentration and default data); balances, allowlists and caps read from the Stellar test network; the decision log written by our policy
Consent She creates her account and is approved by the fund manager; nothing in the mandate is valid until she approves its hash; every Red operation needs her signature on a decoded card; the interface labels test assets and simulated data
Minimisation The model receives her messages and typed tool results only; addresses, secret keys, email addresses and access tokens are removed before every call and the call is aborted if any remain; the agent only learns "eligible" or "not eligible" from the zero-knowledge tier; no identity document is collected
Storage PostgreSQL (accounts with bcrypt password hashes, mandates, orders, the append-only decision log); agent signing keys encrypted with AES-256-GCM (key derived by HKDF-SHA256 from a server secret), never logged, returned or sent to the model; the agent service has no database: sessions live in memory and in local session files ignored by git; secrets only in environment variables, never in the repository
Deletion The prototype has no self-service deletion. Accounts and sessions can be removed by the operator. The decision log is append-only by design (a database trigger refuses UPDATE, DELETE and TRUNCATE) because it is the audit trail; on-chain transactions are permanent and public by nature of the ledger

Section 04

04Known Risks

Risk, impact, mitigation and planned improvement

The known limitations open this section, as in every document; the last column of the table maps each risk to them.

Known limitations (identical in every document, the video and the submission form)

  1. Test assets. All USDC and fund units in this submission are test assets on the Stellar public test network and have no monetary value, except the mainnet transactions described in item 14. Transaction hashes prove the mechanism, not a market.
  2. Our contract, not audited. The mandate wallet is a contract we wrote. It is public and has no upgrade function, but it has not been audited. A bug in it would be our bug.
  3. Agent key custody. The agent's signing key is held by our server, separate from the language model, which never sees it. If the server were compromised, the attacker could only make payments our wallet contract allows: to fund wallets the investor approved, within the per purchase, per period and per fund caps she signed. The contract refuses a decision value it has already used, but it cannot check that a new value matches our decision log; that check is made afterwards by our verification script.
  4. Within the mandate, the agent chooses. Our wallet contract enforces where money can go and how much; it does not judge whether a purchase inside those limits was wise.
  5. Simulated compliance. KYC/KYB, suitability and the jurisdiction perimeter are simulated checks, designed with reference to FINRA 2111, ESMA35-43-3172 and CSRC suitability measures. Funds and debtors are simulated; concentration and default data are synthetic.
  6. Zero-knowledge eligibility. The tier proof shows that the total an investor has invested on this platform reaches a threshold, without revealing it. It is not a regulatory investor status. The platform attests the committed total, and the circuit's phase 2 trusted setup has a single contribution, so whoever controls the setup or the attestation could forge a tier.
  7. Perimeter. The system is not offered to, and is not designed for, persons or entities in Mainland China. We are aware of the February 2026 notice (Yinfa [2026] No. 42) that prohibits RWA tokenization onshore except on designated financial infrastructure with approval. Nothing in this submission is an offer or solicitation to buy or sell securities or fund units in any jurisdiction.
  8. Discretionary management and key custody without a licence. Executing within a mandate is discretionary portfolio management, and holding the agent's signing key is a form of custody. This prototype holds no licence; a production deployment would require licensed partners (fund administrator, custodian, regulated on/off-ramp, key custody) and local authorisation in each target market.
  9. Recurring purchases. Recurring purchases are pre-authorised within the investor's mandate and started by a manual "run recurring now" trigger for the demo. The time trigger is simulated; the on-chain transactions are real.
  10. No return promise. The agent never states or implies a guaranteed or expected return. The demonstration funds state no return figure; every fund is shown with a loss scenario.
  11. What the language model sees. Our agent service sends the LLM provider (Anthropic, Claude) the investor's chat messages and typed tool results: mandate fields, including countries of residence; fund facts; policy outcomes; transaction hashes. Fund texts written by third parties are sent marked as untrusted data. Before every call the service removes Stellar account and contract addresses, secret keys, email addresses and access tokens, and aborts the call if any remain. No private key and no identity document is sent; amounts the investor types in the chat are sent as she typed them.
  12. Composite persona. The investor persona is a composite persona, built from public data. It was not validated by interviews.
  13. Contract refusals. When our wallet contract refuses a transaction, the error code we show (Error(Contract, #N)) comes from the RPC's diagnostic events, which are not part of consensus. What the ledger guarantees is the transaction hash, the FAILED result and the fee charged.
  14. Mainnet slice. One mandate wallet was deployed on Stellar mainnet and funded with less than US$5 in USDC; the agent made one purchase from it to a fund wallet controlled by the team. These are the only mainnet transactions of the mechanism; the team's other mainnet operations were account setup (XLM funding and USDC trustlines). Everything else runs on the Stellar test network. The fund unit token on mainnet is a test asset issued by the team; the purchase proposal there was entered by the operator and decided by our policy code.
